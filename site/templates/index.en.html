      The challenge

      Close

      Every day, real documents end up in AI assistants

      Contracts, HR files, medical reports, bank statements: they are pasted into AI assistants to be summarised, translated or analysed.

      Usually in good faith, and without knowing the rules. Personal data then lands with a third party, often with no legal basis or processing agreement, sometimes outside the European Union: exactly what the GDPR regulates. Once sent, it does not come back.

      The challenge comes down to three demands that pull against each other: keep the logic of the text, so the AI stays useful; make any way back impossible; stay fast and frugal enough never to be bypassed. Hence a workspace, not a page: drop, take back, move on, all day long.

      The story

      Close

      Guillaume annoys Nadège

      Hiding the names is not enough: the AI must still understand who does what.

      Original

      Guillaume annoys Nadège. Nadège hits Guillaume.

      Naive masking

      [NAME] annoys [NAME]. [NAME] hits [NAME]. Who hit whom? An AI can only guess.

      GDPR Guardian

      [PERSON_1] annoys [PERSON_2]. [PERSON_2] hits [PERSON_1]. The roles survive, the identities do not.

      On the workspace’s example, version 1.0.0 turns “Jean Dupont”, “Mr Dupont” and “DUPONT Jean” into one placeholder, [PERSON_1]; “Ms Claire Martin” becomes Ms [PERSON_2]. The title stays, as it helps to follow the text. The invoice number and the amount stay too: they are not personal data.

      Not always, though: of the 2,463 people in the synthetic corpus, 92.1% get a single placeholder; 194 are split across several and 44 share another person’s.

      Each document is anonymised separately, with its own key, destroyed afterwards: [PERSON_1] in one document is not necessarily the same person in another. Later, on the roadmap: processing a set of documents under one code, to keep the same placeholders from one document to the next, still with no way back (the set’s key destroyed at the end).

      Architecture

      Close

      Online or on your machine: same software

      Same package, same version: online to start without installing anything, on your own computer so that GDPR Guardian sends nothing. Open to everyone without a key, the service guards against abuse with a rate limiter that lives in memory only.

      Online

      1. Browser, AI agent, scriptREST or MCP over HTTPS, no key; this workspace calls the same API.
      2. TLS 1.2/1.3 · nginxOVH, in France: firewall, automatic updates, no core dumps, no access log.
      3. Unix socketin a memory-backed folder: no port open between nginx and the service.
      4. Container with no network at allread-only, no privileges.
      5. Front door and rate limiterthe IP address immediately becomes a keyed pseudonym (key renewed every 24 hours), held in memory, never written, forgotten after 10 idle minutes.
      6. A fresh process per requestisolated, memory capped, killed at its deadline; it cannot start anything.
      7. Resultin the response, the same way back, nowhere else. Agents’ deferred uploads: readable with a random 256-bit identifier given to the requester alone, deleted once read or after 10 minutes.
      8. Site daemona datagram without content (kind, format, size, duration, success, origin) feeds the live figures.

      On your computer

      1. Original documentin a folder you allowed; it does not move.
      2. GDPR Guardian, localsame Rust engine, no connection during processing; fully local option: a Docker container with no network.
      3. AI client, over MCP stdioClaude Code, Claude Desktop, Cowork, VS Code or Cursor: it passes the path and receives the anonymised Markdown.
      4. AI modelreceives the anonymised text. GDPR Guardian sends nothing and asks the assistant never to open the original.

      Irreversible by construction

      • One key per document, then destroyed. 256 random bits; mentions of one person are matched through keyed BLAKE2b digests, never through their value. The key is never written; online, it disappears with the request’s process.
      • No mapping table. Nothing ties a placeholder to a name, neither on your side nor on the server: nothing to steal, nothing to restore, not even for us.
      • Explainable. No LLM: every placeholder comes from a named rule (check digits, keyword, form label, your organisation’s own rule).

      Protocol and scores

      How we grade ourselves

      An index is only worth something if anyone can recompute it. Its definition is fixed before the first measurement; the protocol will go through the real service and publish everything: corpora, commands, dated raw results, weaknesses included.

      Confidence index
      Share of documents in which no direct identifier stays readable: a single character of an annotated mention left visible, and the document counts as failed. Computed per corpus family, then averaged with equal weight; 95% interval by stratified resampling of documents (2,000 draws, fixed seed).
      French scope
      Seven families: French synthetic (multi-seed generator, with decoys), red-team mutations, BODACC, BOAMP, ParlaMint-FR, old press (Europeana and HIPE NewsEye, one family), MAPA FR. Volume buys no weight: a million synthetic documents count as one family.
      Published separately, prominently
      Other languages (MAPA DE, ES, IT, NL, PT; MEDDOCAN; German LER; English TAB) and the Judilibre negative control, for precision only.
      On the production server
      Through its local socket, at low priority: without TLS, nginx or the rate limiter. Every run counts in the usage figures and shows apart in the instruments.

      Known limits, measured

      • The barrier lowers the risk, it does not remove it, and it does much worse on real texts (table above); in BODACC company notices, only 5% of the RCS numbers are masked.
      • All these measurements are on text: the conversion of PDF, Word and Excel files is not measured, and a scanned page (an image, with no text) is dropped, with no character recognition.
      • Through the API, a country chosen without France masks neither names nor addresses: 0% on real German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Portuguese texts; with France added, 22 to 50% of mentions (of names: 23 to 41% in legal documents, 95% in Spanish clinical cases).
      • Capitalised common words pass for names (the French title word “Avenant” became a person placeholder): on court decisions, 32% of the masks drawn at random were mistakes.
      • French bank details (RIB) written as separate fields are never recognised; a number broken by a tab or a line break almost always gets through (0% and 4.5% masked), and so does an e-mail written “&#64;”, “%40”, “[dot]” or “(point)” (0%).
      • 27.3% of the company identifiers that get masked are masked by mistake.
      • Context (job title, place, date, a rare event) can allow indirect re-identification: no automated detection prevents that.

A text already pasted into the chat has already left: anonymize_text cannot bring it back.

      On your computer

      For very sensitive documents: the same software, as a local MCP server or in a container with no network; nothing leaves through GDPR Guardian. Installation: local version guide; questions: contact@gardienrgpd.fr.

      Limits of the public service

      Per client: one request processed at a time and two waiting, about 20 seconds of compute in advance, refilled at 0.5 s per second. Your network (/24 in IPv4, /48 in IPv6) shares a budget twice as large and 12 requests in flight. Beyond that: a 429 and a delay to honour (Retry-After), which this workspace applies by itself while sending one document at a time; a saturated server (estimated wait over 10 s): 503; an upload too slow (15 s, plus 128 KiB per second of body): 408.

      Size: 50 MB per file, one million characters per text; per document, 12.5 million characters of result, 10,000 pages or slides, one million HTML tags (opening and closing), 100,000 Word paragraphs (table cells included). Very dense plain text, CSV or spreadsheets reach that ceiling before 50 MB (around 13 MB of text, 16 MB of CSV, 4 MB of spreadsheet): 413, naming the limit. An illustrated 50 MB document takes a few seconds; each may take 60 s plus 1 s per 250 KB (4 min 30 s at 50 MB).

      Privacy

      Close

      No document kept, neither here nor on the server

      • Here. No cookie, no browser storage (neither local nor session storage, nor IndexedDB), no tracker, no third-party resource. Your texts and results live only in this tab’s memory: “Clear everything” empties it, and so does closing the tab.
      • On the server. Each document is processed in memory in an isolated process, and nothing of it remains once the response has left; only the result comes back, to you alone. The file name does not travel: only its extension.
      • We are still a third party. You hand us the text, with no processing agreement. To send nothing: the local version.
      • IP address, in memory only, never written to disk, no access log. The web server keeps it as is (IPv4, or its /64 in IPv6) in its anti-abuse counters, which it recycles as traffic flows. The service replaces it at once with a keyed pseudonym, forgotten after 10 idle minutes (key renewed every 24 hours). To count visitors once a day: a keyed digest of the truncated address (/24, or /48 in IPv6) and of the browser’s user agent, key and digests forgotten every day.
      • Usage statistics: kind of operation, format, size, duration, success, origin; never the content, the file name or the IP address.
      • Legal basis: legitimate interest in providing, protecting and securing the service (GDPR, Art. 6(1)(f) and 32). Questions: contact@gardienrgpd.fr; complaints: the CNIL, France’s data protection authority.
