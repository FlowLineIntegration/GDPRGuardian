# ner-models

### Key takeaways
- No permissively licensed model is strong at French PII out of the box. On a French PII set, gliner_multi_pii-v1 scores 48.9 F1, SauerkrautLM-GLiNER 44.3 and gliner_multi-v2.1 24.1. On the multilingual SPY benchmark, F1 sits around 0.40–0.48. Regex, checksum validators and gazetteers must therefore carry the structured identifiers, and NER should only cover names, organisations and addresses.
- Best clean-provenance multilingual default: urchade/gliner_multi_pii-v1. Apache-2.0, built on mDeBERTa-v3-base, covers en/fr/de/es/pt/it, trained on Apache-licensed data generated with Mistral. Limits: max_len 384 words and max_types 25, so chunking is required.
- For French PER/ORG/LOC the clean choices are the CamemBERT models. cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner is MIT, 67.5M params, about 43 ms per call on a laptop CPU, with an ONNX int8 file. Jean-Baptiste/camembert-ner is MIT, 110M params, PER F1 0.948. Both are trained on WikiNER, which is CC BY 4.0.
- A license tag of Apache/MIT does not prove clean provenance. The OpenMed French PII models (Apache tag) and the MIT CamemBERT PII fine-tunes (Spyridon; Anonym-IA, likely) are trained on AI4Privacy data, whose license is academic and non-commercial only. Piiranha is CC-BY-NC-ND, GLiNER v0/v1 are CC-BY-NC, WikiNEuRal is CC-BY-NC-SA, and NVIDIA's model uses a custom license.
- Several 2025–2026 newcomers fit the license rule but carry caveats. fastino gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi (Apache, 7 EU languages including French, best SPY F1 0.477) was trained on data generated by GPT-5.4 and has low precision (0.35–0.37). The knowledgator gliner-x models (Apache, mT5, 23 languages, 0.5B/0.9B params) use data annotated by an unnamed LLM, and that dataset has no license.
- The knowledgator PII v1.0 family (edge/small/base/large) and every gliner-bi-v2.0 bi-encoder use English-only encoders (Ettin, DeBERTa-v3, bge-en). The 'multilingual' tag on them should not be trusted for French.
- Two candidates break the no-LLM rule. gliner-stream-pii-v1.0 uses a Qwen3-0.6B causal backbone. openai/privacy-filter (Apache, 1.5B params with 50M active, 8 labels) is a converted autoregressive checkpoint and mainly English. Use them only as benchmark references.
- Uni-encoder GLiNER packs the labels into the same input as the text, so cost grows with label count: in the GLiNER2 paper, CPU latency rose from 130 to 208 ms going from 5 to 50 labels. The gliner docs advise a uni-encoder up to about 50 types and a bi-encoder (with precomputed label embeddings) beyond that. We need fewer than 10 labels, so a uni-encoder is the right choice.
- Quantization method matters for recall. Dynamic INT8 on GLiNER-small cut latency from 167 to 59 ms but dropped recall from 70.9% to 53.1%. Weight-only 8-bit (MatMulNBits) on gliner_multi_pii-v1 matched FP16 on 162 of 164 decisions, with peak memory of 309 MB versus 1,737 MB.
- Expected CPU latency: about 40–90 ms per chunk for distil/base CamemBERT, and about 60–250 ms per chunk for mDeBERTa-base GLiNER/GLiNER2. Example: about 250 ms p50 for GLiNER2 with an INT8 encoder on 4 vCPU AMD. These are self-reported figures on different hardware; we must benchmark on our own servers.
- For the Excel question: NER on isolated spreadsheet cells has almost no context and its accuracy drops. Handle spreadsheets column by column: classify each column from its header keywords plus regex/validator hit rates on sampled cells. Run NER only on free-text cells or columns typed as names, feeding it 'header: value' strings in batches, and give the same value the same placeholder.

### Recommendations
- Division of labour: regex plus checksum validators own every structured identifier (NIR mod-97, SIREN/SIRET Luhn, IBAN mod-97, cards, email, phone, IP). NER is asked only for about 4–8 English lowercase labels: person, organization, address/street address, city, and optionally date of birth. Fewer labels means faster runs, more room in the window and fewer false positives.
- Fast tier: regex, gazetteers and context words, plus cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner (ONNX) for French. For other EU languages, gliner_multi_pii-v1 rebuilt with weight-only int8, called with at most 4 labels (or regex-only when latency SLOs are tight).
- Balanced tier (default): regex plus gliner_multi_pii-v1 (weight-only int8 ONNX, about 300 MB resident). For French, take the union with distilcamembert's PER spans to raise name recall.
- Accurate tier: regex plus a span-vote union of gliner_multi_pii-v1 (fp32), camembert-ner (French), and gliner-x-base/large or GLiNER2-PII. The latter two only after legal sign-off on their LLM-generated training data. Never include gliner-stream-pii or openai/privacy-filter (no-LLM rule).
- Per language: French uses CamemBERT plus GLiNER. de/es/it/pt use gliner_multi_pii-v1 (trained languages). nl and other EU languages use E3-JSI multi-pii-domains or gliner-x, plus regex packs. 'auto' runs language ID per chunk first (choose a permissively licensed library).
- Merging policy: validated regex beats NER, which beats unvalidated regex. Resolve overlaps by preferring the longer span, then the higher score. Use per-label thresholds starting at 0.5 (GLiNER default); test 0.3–0.4 for person on a French dev set, since recall matters more than precision for anonymization. Add a stop-list filter for pronouns and common nouns, a known GLiNER failure mode.
- Chunking: sentence-aware chunks of at most about 300 words for mDeBERTa GLiNER (max_len 384 words, labels share the window), with a 50-character overlap. Deduplicate spans across chunks, group chunks by length into batches, and pin ORT threads.
- Quantization: use weight-only 8-bit (MatMulNBits) or FP32. Never ship dynamic activation INT8 without a recall test: GLiNER-small recall fell 17.8 points with it.
- Supply chain: pin every model to a Hugging Face commit hash, verify SHA256, keep a mirror on EU storage, and load safetensors or ONNX only (the privacy-filter typosquatting incident shows the risk). Write a MODEL_LICENSES.md documenting the license of both weights and data.
- Build a manually annotated French eval set (chat, email, contracts, HR, medical, plus spreadsheet cells) under a permissive license. Track per-type recall and the residual leak rate, and report SPY/REDACT scores for comparability. Do not trust in-distribution model-card F1.
- Own-model roadmap with no LLM in data generation: (a) generate French text from Faker fr_FR templates plus validator-valid NIR/SIRET/IBAN; (b) add WikiNER (CC BY 4.0) and the Gretel/Kiji/E3-JSI/urchade synthetic sets; (c) fine-tune gliner_multi_pii-v1 to keep zero-shot ability, plus a CamemBERTav2 or mmBERT-small token classifier; (d) optionally distill from our own encoder ensemble into the small model. Publish the result under Apache-2.0 with a data card.
- Excel: yes, handle it, but as columns rather than prose. Detect each column's type from header keywords (FR/EN dictionaries) plus regex/validator hit rates on sampled cells, then mask whole columns. Run NER only on free-text or name-typed columns, as 'header: value' batches. Cache by value so repeated values get the same placeholder and are inferred only once.

### Risks
- Low French recall: on French PII, the permissive zero-shot models score only 24–49 F1, and REDACT puts GLiNER at 0.32 F1. Names in short or informal text, and in table cells, will leak unless regex, gazetteers and an ensemble compensate.
- License laundering: many 'Apache/MIT' PII models are trained on data under non-commercial terms (AI4Privacy) or on outputs from OpenAI or Llama models. Bundling them could expose an MIT project to claims or reputational risk.
- Over-masking: GLiNER2-PII has 0.35–0.37 precision and tags common nouns as names. Excessive placeholders degrade the LLM answers that downstream users depend on.
- Window limits: the gliner_multi family has max_len 384 words and max_types 25. Long labels lists and missing chunk overlap cause silent misses at chunk boundaries.
- Memory: mDeBERTa's 250k-token vocabulary means about 1.7 GB peak in FP16 ONNX per process. Multiple workers per host multiply this unless we use weight-only int8 or vocabulary pruning.
- Dynamic INT8 quantization can silently cut recall by about 18 points.
- Benchmarks in model cards are self-reported and in-distribution: Spyridon falls from 0.94 to 0.65 out of distribution. CPU latency figures come from heterogeneous hardware.
- Model-hub supply chain: typosquatted repositories, and unpinned revisions that can change behaviour.

### Open questions
- Does writing GLiNER labels in French ('personne', 'adresse') beat English labels on French text? No published comparison exists. The model cards' examples use English labels on French text; we need an internal A/B test.
- Will the project's legal policy accept models whose training data was generated with OpenAI (GPT-4o, GPT-5.4) or Llama outputs? This affects GLiNER2-PII, gliner2-multi and the knowledgator bi-v2 models.
- Could not verify (HTTP 401): TheoDB/privacy-filter-fr (a French fine-tune of openai/privacy-filter), the OpenMed French EuroMed-210M card, and the VAGOsolutions French PII benchmark license. The Stanza model license is also unconfirmed.
- Actual CPU latency and throughput on our target servers (vCPU count, AVX-512/AMX) for distilcamembert, gliner_multi_pii-v1 (q8w) and gliner-x-base all remain to be benchmarked.
- Is it worth pruning mDeBERTa's 250k vocabulary to EU-language tokens to cut memory? Not evaluated in the sources found.
- The shared web-search budget ran out during this research, so some 2026 newcomers (e.g. further French GLiNER fine-tunes) may be missing. A follow-up search pass is advisable.
- Excel: should column-type detection be its own deterministic module (header dictionaries plus validators) that skips NER for typed columns? This needs a decision alongside the document-conversion scope.

### Critical claims
- urchade/gliner_multi_pii-v1 is Apache-2.0, fine-tuned from gliner_multi-v2.1 on urchade/synthetic-pii-ner-mistral-v1, with language metadata en, fr, de, es, pt, it [https://huggingface.co/urchade/gliner_multi_pii-v1/raw/main/README.md]
- gliner_multi_pii-v1 uses microsoft/mdeberta-v3-base with max_len 384 and max_types 25 [https://huggingface.co/urchade/gliner_multi_pii-v1/blob/main/gliner_config.json]
- GLiNER v0/v1 models (gliner_base, gliner_multi, *-v1) are CC-BY-NC-4.0, while v2/v2.1 including gliner_multi-v2.1 are Apache-2.0 [https://huggingface.co/urchade/gliner_large-v2.1]
- knowledgator gliner-pii v1.0 backbones are English encoders: ettin-encoder-32m (edge), ettin-encoder-68m (small), deberta-v3-small (base), deberta-v3-large (large). Reported F1 on synthetic-multi-pii-ner-v1: 75.50 / 76.84 / 80.99 / 83.25, vs 76.86 for gliner_multi_pii-v1 [https://huggingface.co/knowledgator/gliner-pii-base-v1.0]
- fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi is Apache-2.0 and covers 7 languages including French. It was trained on 4,910 synthetic texts generated by GPT-5.4, with SPY avg F1 0.477 vs 0.398 (gliner_multi_pii-v1) and 0.380 (openai/privacy-filter) [https://huggingface.co/fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi]
- OpenMed French PII models are tagged Apache-2.0 but trained on the AI4Privacy PII Masking 400k French subset [https://huggingface.co/OpenMed/OpenMed-PII-French-SuperClinical-Small-44M-v1]
- The AI4Privacy pii-masking-400k license grants access exclusively for academic and non-commercial purposes and requires a written license for redistribution or derivative works [https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4privacy/pii-masking-400k/blob/main/license.md]

- cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner is MIT, 67.5M params, and averages 43.44 ms on an AMD Ryzen 5 4500U vs 314.96 ms for flair/ner-french [https://huggingface.co/cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner]

- knowledgator/gliner-stream-pii-v1.0 is built on Qwen3-0.6B (a causal LLM) and scores 10.91 strict F1 on mapa-eur-lex [https://huggingface.co/knowledgator/gliner-stream-pii-v1.0]
- spaCy fr_core_news_lg is licensed LGPL-LR, with NER F 0.839 [https://huggingface.co/spacy/fr_core_news_lg]

### Verifier notes
- Web search budget ran out early, so all checks used direct fetches of primary pages (HF cards and configs, PyPI, GitHub, arXiv). EUR-Lex and curia pages returned empty or redirected, so no legal claim was verified. The 'privacy-filter typosquatting incident' cited under supply chain is unverified.
- Inconsistent provenance standard: urchade synthetic-pii-ner-mistral-v1, pile-mistral-v0.1 and E3-JSI are all LLM-generated (the E3-JSI card says 'synthetic (LLM-generated)', model unnamed) yet are graded 'ok'. Fastino (GPT-5.4), Kiji (unnamed LLMs) and gliner-x are graded 'caution'. The recommended default gliner_multi_pii-v1, and its parent gliner_multi-v2.1 (Pile text annotated by Mistral, empty dataset card), carry the same class of risk. Either grade them all 'caution' or explain that the real issue is the generator's terms of service, for example OpenAI's ToS versus Apache-licensed Mistral open weights.
- Self-contradiction in the own-model roadmap: it promises 'no LLM in data generation', but step (b) adds the Gretel, Kiji, E3-JSI and urchade sets, which are all LLM-generated, and step (c) fine-tunes gliner_multi_pii-v1, itself trained on Mistral-generated data. Only Faker templates and WikiNER are LLM-free.
- nvidia/Nemotron-PII is English-only (100k English records, NeMo Data Designer), so it adds little for French. Its card also refers to unnamed NVIDIA 'terms of service' on top of CC BY 4.0.
- Omission on English-vocabulary encoders: the takeaway warns about knowledgator's English encoders but misses that DataikuNLP/kiji-pii-model and OpenMed-PII-French-SuperClinical-Small are built on microsoft/deberta-v3-small, also an English-vocabulary encoder.
- GLiNER2-PII (fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi) actually uses a microsoft/mdeberta-v3-base encoder (config.json, vocab 250112, 1.23 GB fp32), not the 205M English GLiNER2 base the card text suggests. A related newer checkpoint, fastino/GLiNER2-Guardrails-PII-Multi (Apache-2.0, papers dated May 2026), fine-tunes fastino/gliner2-base-v1 and adds WildGuardTrain data; it is not covered by the review.
- The GLiNER2 latency figures (130 to 208 ms for 5 to 50 labels) are from a text-classification benchmark (Table 4), not NER. Keep the qualitative point but drop or requalify the numbers.
- Checksum omissions for France: SIRET has the La Poste exception (digit sum divisible by 5). Other French identifiers worth adding to the regex/validator layer: numéro fiscal (SPI, 13 digits, first digit 0-3, check = first 10 digits mod 511) and TVA intracom FR (key = (SIREN*100 + 12) mod 97, plus a new-style alphanumeric key variant). python-stdnum, the reference implementation, is LGPL-2.1+, not MIT, so GDPR Guardian should reimplement these validators rather than depend on it if strict MIT compatibility is required.
- Quantization note: distilcamembert-base-ner ships model_quantized.onnx, most likely Optimum dynamic quantization. Under the review's own rule ('never ship dynamic activation INT8 without a recall test') that file must be recall-tested, or re-quantized weight-only, before use in the fast tier. The GLiNER README also says INT8 needs QAT to keep accuracy. blinzl q8w is a third-party upload, updated 2 days before this check, and its 162/164 agreement was measured on German notes only. Prefer self-quantizing with ORT MatMulNBits and recording hashes.
- EuroBERT-210m requires trust_remote_code=True, which conflicts with the supply-chain recommendation (safetensors/ONNX only, pinned commits). Flag it or exclude it from the backbone shortlist.
- Version updates: spaCy fr_core_news_lg 3.8.0 is current (NER F 84.18; the HF card shows 3.7.0). gliner is at 0.2.29 (2026-09-08), pinning transformers <5.17, and gliner2 at 2.1.0 (2026-10-09).
- Excel answer (the user's actual question) is sound in principle: handle spreadsheets column by column using header plus validator hit rates, with consistent placeholders. It misses format-level leak paths, which matter more than NER accuracy here. Hidden or very hidden sheets, cell comments/notes, docProps (author, last modified by), headers/footers, named ranges, data-validation lists, hyperlinks, chart caches, pivot caches (pivotCacheRecords keep a full copy of the source data), external links, embedded objects and images, and orphaned entries in sharedStrings.xml can all keep PII after the visible cells are masked. Formulas that reference masked cells, and the cached formula values, also need handling.
- Excel library licensing: openpyxl 3.1.5 is MIT (last release June 2024, slow maintenance). pyxlsb, used for .xlsb, is LGPLv3+, which is not MIT-compatible for bundling. Consider python-calamine (MIT) for reading. Decide explicitly whether .xls, .xlsb, .ods and .csv are in scope.
- Excel legal omission: tabular data carries quasi-identifier risk (date of birth plus postcode plus sex) that masking direct identifiers does not remove. Replacing values with consistent placeholders is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation, under GDPR Recital 26, WP29 Opinion 05/2014 and EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisation (public consultation Jan to Mar 2025; final status not verified here). The Excel recommendation should say that consistent placeholders produce pseudonymised data, and offer generalisation or suppression of quasi-identifier columns if 'anonymisation' is claimed. The CJEU judgment C-413/23 P (EDPS v SRB, 2025), on whether pseudonymised data is personal data for the recipient, is relevant but could not be fetched for verification.
